# Project Agent Instructions

## New public Earn management (user decision, 2026-11-25)

The independent `/admin/earn/*` module manages the new public site's Financial → Earn (`/NewQuantCapital/earn/simple` and `/earn/my-earnings`), not AI strategies or Quant Fund. Subscription submissions must remain pending review with no holding, interest accrual, or term countdown. Only an explicit admin approval creates the holding and sets its interest-start and maturity timestamps. Rejection must not create a holding or yield. Approval and redemption settlement must reject duplicate processing and leave an operation log. Prototype state is local until backend integration is explicitly implemented. Funding progress uses initialSubscribed plus subscribed (approved subscription principal only); remaining quota and approval capacity checks use that same combined total. Operators enter an initial funding percentage (0–100%), automatically converted into initialSubscribed using total quota. Changing quota retains the percentage and recalculates the initial amount. Initial funding is nonnegative mock configuration; approved amounts are not editable through product saves. Do not link this mock configuration to the public frontend until explicitly requested. Product creation/editing uses a dedicated single-page form with grouped sections, without Tabs or a compact modal. Only fixed-term products show a support-early-redemption switch (off by default for new products); enabling it reveals a fee base (principal or principal plus unsettled yield) and a percentage fee rate. Early redemption uses that fee only, never recalculates yield at a flexible rate. A separate redemptionReviewRequired switch defaults on for both flexible and fixed products. Each new redemption snapshots that setting: manual requests remain pending until explicit approval/settlement or rejection restoring the holding; automatic requests settle with system attribution. Changing the product switch never auto-processes existing pending requests. Fees use the holding’s frozen subscription terms; already paid yield is excluded from the settlement base. Flexible products omit those controls. Preserve existing saved rules and draft values when switching product type. Configure daily-rate lower/upper bounds for future server-side random accrual; preserve public display/APY values. Product saves do not require an operation reason, but still log the before/after configuration. All Earn operations, including subscription approval/rejection, product listing/delisting and redemption settlement, require no operation-reason input. Automatically log the action and state transition; show generated text as operation description, not a user-supplied reason. Audit/settlement lists and details display the reviewer, blank while pending; successful approval/rejection/settlement stores that identity and uses it for the corresponding operation log. Prototype reviewer identities must be explicitly marked as simulated until real authentication/backend integration. Pending orders have a primary review action; completed/rejected orders expose a read-only detail action labeled 详情 and must not be reviewable again. Do not reintroduce repeated explanatory banners on Earn pages or dialogs.

These instructions apply to every agent and every change in this repository.

## Responsive and adaptive interaction constraints

All responsive and adaptive work must preserve the same core business capabilities, data results, authorization, validation, error handling, safety confirmations, and status feedback across PC, tablet, and mobile. A low-frequency or secondary capability may be collapsed, collected, or converted into another component, but it must not be removed and must remain discoverable and accessible; do not use `display: none` to hide a capability users still need.

1. Adapt according to available space, content, input capability, and user preference, not device names or User-Agent alone. Choose a breakpoint when content can no longer remain usable, rather than tying it mechanically to a device model. Component form may change without changing business semantics, submission result, default value, or risk level.
2. A desktop Dialog may become a bottom or full-screen Drawer, and a side Drawer may become a full-width or bottom Drawer. Preserve the title, intentional close path, focus, backdrop, scroll, submission, and error semantics, and apply the applicable Dialog and Drawer constraints. Hover-only information must have click and keyboard access; drag, swipe, long-press, and precision-pointer interactions need a simple click or keyboard alternative.
3. A breakpoint change, window resize, or orientation change must retain unsubmitted form data, filters, searches, pagination, selections, expanded items, upload progress, and business context. When an open overlay changes form, retain exactly one active instance: no duplicate backdrops, requests, callbacks, focus traps, or scroll locks. Move focus to equivalent content or action, or a reasonable title/first action when no equivalent target remains. Never interrupt a submission, upload, or uninterruptible operation.
4. Support desktop, laptop, tablet, narrow and landscape phones, low-height viewports, 200% browser zoom, system text enlargement, dynamic browser chrome, virtual keyboard, and all `safe-area-inset-*` directions. Core content and actions must remain reachable without a key flow requiring two-dimensional scrolling. Fixed headers, footers, and floating actions must not fully obscure the focused element, error message, or primary action; dynamic viewport units need a reasonable fallback and scrolling must remain clear and predictable.
5. Do not assume desktop means mouse-only or mobile means touch-only. Core actions must work with keyboard, mouse, and touch, retain a visible focus indicator, and be usable without precision tapping. Keyboard Focus must expose hover-supplemented information; an interactive floating surface must remain usable when the pointer moves into it.
6. Prioritize titles, amounts, states, errors, and primary actions. Support long headings, words, translated text, and enlarged fonts without losing meaning or access to an action. Do not convey important information only by icon, placement, color, or hover; use descriptive names for collapsed entries.
7. For each change, verify the applicable viewport, input, orientation, zoom, virtual keyboard, safe-area, reduced-motion/high-contrast, long-text, and active-state breakpoint cases. Any interaction, input method, or viewport not actually checked must be reported as unverified with the required check; never state that an unperformed check passed.

The full cross-platform standard is [responsive-adaptive.md](https://github.com/gloopai/frontend-product-interaction-standards/blob/main/references/responsive-adaptive.md). Apply it together with the component-specific rules; where compatible rules differ in strictness, follow the stricter rule.

## Searchable single-select constraints

These requirements apply to custom, searchable, single-select controls whose business value must come from an existing option. They do not cover multi-select, tags, free-text creation, trees, cascaders, date pickers, or complex options with independent interactive controls. The full standard is [selects-comboboxes.md](https://github.com/gloopai/frontend-product-interaction-standards/blob/main/references/selects-comboboxes.md); the requirements below remain independently enforceable.

1. Keep `selectedValue`, `selectedOption`, `query`, `activeOption`, `open`, loading, error, `searchPlacement`, `resolvedPlacement`, and inline `displayText` separate. New commits come only from an existing enabled option. A formerly legal value may become orphaned invalid after refresh: retain raw value/cached label, show invalid, block submission or require reselection by risk, and never silently clear/replace it.
2. Explicit placement wins; explicit `drawer` stays a Drawer on wide screens. `auto` uses stable unfiltered metadata and declared capabilities in order: no-needed-search + small stable set → `none`; constrained/mobile + search/keyboard pressure → `drawer`; must keep value visible → `panel`; declared frequent name/code/UID search → `inline`; otherwise require product configuration. Never use filtered result count or Agent preference. Record/test result and reason; freeze an open session except declared viewport/input/virtual-keyboard changes.
3. `inline` is an Editable Combobox. Closed visible input is committed label/allowed empty display; opening initializes draft query. Any printable, Backspace/Delete, paste, or cut editing intent enters draft, with the first edit replacing the label; selectedValue/displayText remain committed until explicit select/Enter commits. Every non-commit close (outside, Escape, Tab, allowed Drawer close) discards draft/active, restores committed display, and fires no value change.
4. `panel` has a disclosure button with contextual field/current-value/action name, `aria-expanded`, and `aria-controls` to its panel container; do not claim `aria-haspopup="listbox"` unless it controls that Listbox. `drawer` button has `aria-haspopup="dialog"`, `aria-controls` Drawer dialog, and `aria-expanded`. Both open into their contextual inner search Combobox; after close animation set the outer trigger to `aria-expanded="false"` and return focus. IDs remain stable through Portal/mode conversion.
5. Inner panel/drawer search Comboboxes have contextual labels, `role="combobox"`, `aria-autocomplete="list"`, visible-listbox `aria-expanded="true"`, stable Listbox `aria-controls`, and an active descendant only for a rendered DOM option. Inline/select-only primary Comboboxes own business validation with `aria-required="true"` when required and `aria-invalid="true"` plus associated selection error when invalid; panel/drawer external field wrapper/trigger owns field label, committed value, required indication, and selection error. Inner search owns only request errors.
6. `none` exclusively uses a WAI-ARIA Select-only Combobox: visible label/value, `role="combobox"`, expanded, stable Listbox controls, implicit/explicit `aria-haspopup="listbox"`, required/invalid, DOM focus retention, and rendered active descendant. No button+Listbox or fake search input. Space/Enter open when closed; Space and Enter both commit open active and close; type-ahead moves active; Escape discards/closes; Tab closes and continues.
7. Options use stable ID, `role="option"`, and disabled options `aria-disabled="true"`. While open, only active has `aria-selected="true"` and is referenced by active descendant; committed-but-not-active is visual only. With no active all are false and descendant is removed; closed lists expose none. Reconcile active after query/result/mode/render changes: existing enabled rendered active, then enabled rendered committed, then first enabled rendered option, else null.
8. Switching into `none` retains the session query but suspends filtering and shows all options; switching back in the same open session restores filter. Mode conversion retains one instance and state, without duplicate requests/callbacks/overlays/focus traps/scroll locks/animations. Disabled/read-only use native semantics when possible; custom controls use `aria-disabled`/`aria-readonly` and block interaction; disabled options are skipped.
9. Editable modes use Arrow navigation and Enter commit while Home/End, left/right, Backspace/Delete, and platform-modifier keys retain native text/caret behavior. Only select-only `none` uses Home/End option navigation. Focus movement/type-ahead never commits.
10. Drawer Tab/Shift+Tab stays trapped and never closes. PC inline Tab closes only with no other popup control; its retry is a keyboard-reachable adjacent composite button after input and exiting the composite closes. Panel Tab may move among search/status/retry and closes only after leaving the composite. Retry is never inside an option.
11. Local search is immediate; remote search debounces about 250ms and cancels/ignores stale results. Refresh, stale results, failure, or pagination never commit/replace selection, randomly reorder, or leave dangling active IDs. Keep open on failure, expose textual loading/count/empty/error status, and provide reachable retry.
12. PC popup anchors to its mode control, flips/portals within viewport, keeps search/status fixed and only options scroll; use `150ms ease-out`/`100ms ease-in` (reduced motion ≤50ms). Drawer uses its fixed search/title/list layout and complete Drawer semantics. Virtual lists retain active DOM option and scroll it into view; report count/position.
13. Verify all explicit modes; deterministic auto reason/freeze/allowed transition; inline restore; panel/drawer ARIA/focus; select-only keyboard; unique selected/active reconciliation; validation ownership; Tab/retry; caret keys; none query pause; orphaned invalid; disabled states; async/virtual/Portal/mobile/zoom/keyboard/reduced-motion behavior. Report every unverified check.

## Dialog interaction constraints

All dialogs, modals, and dialog-like popups must meet these required acceptance criteria. Preserve them during refactors and verify relevant interactions and viewport sizes; any interaction or viewport behavior not actually tested must be reported as unverified with the required check.

1. Backdrop clicks must never close a dialog or start its closing animation. Do not add backdrop-close handlers; disable the equivalent UI-library option. A dialog closes only through an intentional in-dialog action. The outer frame must remain non-scrolling (`overflow: hidden`): constrain its height and let only the body/content region scroll (`overflow-y: auto`), keeping headers, footers, and actions in the fixed frame. The viewport-fixed overlay (`position: fixed; inset: 0`) must cover the full viewport at the application root when needed, never a local panel, transformed ancestor, or other clipped container.
2. Opening uses an overlay fade and dialog fade/`scale(0.96)` to `scale(1)` over `200ms ease-out`; closing reverses it over `150ms ease-in` and unmounts only after completion. Block duplicate open/close actions during motion, and retain the overlay, focus containment, and scroll lock through the close animation. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, omit scaling and use fades of at most `50ms` or no animation.
3. On open, focus the appropriate in-dialog target: the first reasonable action in ordinary forms, the title/top static element for long content, or the least destructive action for irreversible work. Keep `Tab` and `Shift + Tab` within the active dialog. After the close animation, return focus to the trigger or a logical next-flow target if it no longer exists. `Escape` normally closes only the topmost dialog; while submitting, in an uninterruptible flow, or with unsaved work, disable Escape or require confirmation.
4. While open, background content must be unavailable to pointer, touch, keyboard, and assistive technology through native modal behavior or an equivalent such as `inert`. Use `<dialog>`, or `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal="true"`; provide an accessible name through a visible title and `aria-labelledby` when possible, falling back to `aria-label` only when no visible title exists. Do not put complex full content in `aria-describedby`. Provide a visible, focusable, accessibly named close, cancel, confirm, or equivalent action. Every ordinary dialog that users may exit must also keep a visible close button in its fixed top-right header; footer cancel, close, confirm, or other actions do not replace it. Use a clear close icon with `aria-label="关闭"`, keyboard focus, and an adequate pointer/touch target; it must remain visible while content scrolls, titles wrap, and layouts adapt to mobile or low-height viewports, and it must follow the same safe close path as cancel. Hide or disable it only when submission is explicitly uninterruptible, confirmation is mandatory, a security flow forbids exit, or another documented business rule disallows closing; agents and developers must not omit it by preference.
5. With layered dialogs, only the topmost dialog is interactive; every lower layer and the page background stay inert. On closing the top layer, restore focus to its trigger in the underlying dialog. Use a shared layer system rather than arbitrary local `z-index` values.
6. During async work, prevent duplicate submission, close, and callback execution. Loading controls retain accessible names and expose needed state to assistive technology. On failure, keep the dialog open, present a textual error, and move focus or provide an error summary at a sensible location; never auto-close before the error can be read. If interruption is disallowed, clearly disable the relevant closing paths and communicate that state.
7. Controls and focused elements must remain reachable at zoom, on mobile, in low-height viewports, and with a virtual keyboard; they must not be fully hidden by fixed headers, footers, or the keyboard. Avoid fixed pixel heights and provide fallbacks for dynamic viewport units. On close, route change, or unmount, clear scroll lock, `inert`, focus traps, event listeners, and animation state. Reopening must not retain stale overlays, loading, errors, or closing state.

Recommended design: keep destructive-action button order stable, use task-specific titles rather than generic “Notice” or “Information,” clear old loading and error state on reopen unless intentionally retained, and avoid complex navigation inside simple confirmations. See the [WAI-ARIA modal dialog pattern](https://www.w3.org/WAI/ARIA/apg/patterns/dialog-modal/), [MDN dialog element guidance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTML/Reference/Elements/dialog), and the [full Dialog interaction standard](https://github.com/gloopai/frontend-product-interaction-standards/blob/main/references/dialogs.md).

## Drawer interaction constraints

All modal Drawers, Sheets, and slide-out panels from the left, right, top, or bottom must meet these required acceptance criteria. Preserve them during refactors and verify relevant interactions and viewport sizes; any interaction or viewport behavior not actually tested must be reported as unverified with the required check. Ordinary non-modal sidebars, fixed navigation, and page splits without an overlay relationship are not modal Drawers.

1. A modal Drawer uses a viewport-fixed backdrop (`position: fixed; inset: 0`) at the application root or an equivalent unclipped layer. It must cover the full viewport, never a local panel, positioned ancestor, or transformed container. Backdrop clicks must never close the Drawer or start its closing animation; disable equivalent UI-library behavior. Drag, swipe, and drag-handle gestures must never close it. Except for the `Escape` keyboard path in item 4, when that path is allowed by the business state, a Drawer closes only through an intentional in-Drawer close button, cancel, confirm, or explicit business action; backdrop, drag, and swipe are never closing paths.
2. The Drawer frame remains non-scrolling (`overflow: hidden`): constrain its available size and let only its content region scroll (`overflow-y: auto`). Keep the header, fixed top-right close button, footer actions, and necessary status region in the frame so they cannot scroll out of view. Every ordinary Drawer users may exit must provide an always-visible, focusable close icon in its fixed header top-right with `aria-label="关闭"`, adequate pointer/touch target, and the same safe close path as cancel. Footer actions do not replace it. Hide or disable it only for an explicitly uninterruptible submission, mandatory confirmation, security flow, or documented business rule, and communicate why exit is unavailable.
3. Left Drawers are fixed to the left edge and enter left-to-right, then exit to the left; right Drawers mirror that behavior. Top Drawers are fixed to the top edge and enter top-to-bottom, then exit upward; bottom Drawers mirror that behavior. Open with backdrop fade plus edge-aligned translation over `200ms ease-out`; close with the reversed translation and fade over `150ms ease-in`, unmounting only after completion. Block duplicate opens, closes, and callbacks during motion and retain backdrop, focus containment, background isolation, and scroll lock through the close animation. With `prefers-reduced-motion: reduce`, omit translation and use fades of at most `50ms`, or no animation.
4. On open, focus the title/top static element for long content, the first reasonable action for ordinary forms, or the least destructive option for irreversible work. Keep `Tab` and `Shift + Tab` within the topmost Drawer. `Escape` normally closes only the topmost Drawer; while submitting, in an uninterruptible flow, or with unsaved work, disable Escape or require confirmation. After the close animation, return focus to the trigger or a logical next-flow target if that trigger no longer exists.
5. While a modal Drawer is open, background content must be unavailable to pointer, touch, keyboard, and assistive technology through native modal behavior or an equivalent such as `inert`, and page scrolling must be locked. Use native modal behavior, or `role="dialog"` with `aria-modal="true"` and an accessible name, preferably a visible title linked by `aria-labelledby`; use `aria-label` only with no visible title. Do not put complex full content in `aria-describedby`. Non-modal Drawers must not claim `aria-modal="true"` or isolate the background without cause.
6. With layered Drawers, or a Drawer layered with a Dialog, only the topmost layer is interactive; every lower layer and the page background remain inert. On closing the top layer, restore focus to its trigger in the underlying layer. Use a shared layer system rather than arbitrary local `z-index` values.
7. During async work, prevent duplicate submission, close, and callback execution. Loading controls retain accessible names and expose needed state to assistive technology. On failure, keep the Drawer open, present a textual error, and move focus or provide an error summary at a sensible location; never auto-close before the error can be read. If interruption is disallowed, clearly disable every relevant closing path and communicate that state.
8. Constrain left/right Drawer width and top/bottom Drawer height without relying on fixed pixel heights that hide content or actions. Ensure controls and focus targets stay reachable on mobile, at zoom, in low-height and dynamic viewports, and with a virtual keyboard. Respect the corresponding `safe-area-inset-top`, `safe-area-inset-bottom`, `safe-area-inset-left`, and `safe-area-inset-right` regions.
9. Before activating a new Drawer instance, clear the prior instance's stale overlay, loading, error, closing, and animation state, plus listeners or effects that should no longer exist. While the current Drawer is open, establish and retain its required scroll lock, background isolation (including `inert`), focus trap, and listeners; reopening must not clear these current valid protections. Only after the current close animation completes, or on route change or unmount, clear its scroll lock, `inert`, focus traps, listeners, animation state, and temporary loading, error, and closing state. Verify all four directions, backdrop/drag/swipe resistance, fixed-frame scrolling, close controls, focus, Escape, semantics, background isolation, layering, async errors, reduced motion, safe-area behavior, and cleanup. Report any untested interaction or viewport behavior as unverified and state the required check.

## No highlighted selection/focus borders (user decision, 2026-11-25)

Do not add highlighted borders, outlines, outer focus rings, focus shadows or double borders for selected, focused or expanded controls/cards. This includes green accent rings and gray/white replacement rings. Keep ordinary structural borders unchanged across these states. Use background, text weight/color, check marks or underlines to distinguish selection and keyboard focus without an outer ring. Preserve keyboard operation, ARIA, validation messages and a visible non-border focus cue. Apply this convention to subsequent UI work, including library default focus styles; never reintroduce rings as an accessibility substitute when a surface/text cue can serve that purpose. Implementation (2026-11-27): the admin and legacy front share one cue in `src/styles/theme.css` — Ant/library outlines and focus shadows are off (`controlOutlineWidth: 0`, resting border kept, error/warning borders kept), fields get a light background wash, buttons/links/tabs/`tabindex` targets get an underline plus wash; forced-colors mode keeps a system outline. ## Operation feedback must use toast (user decision, 2026-11-25)

Applies to every public, legacy frontend, admin and agent-system page, form, Dialog and Drawer. Success/failure feedback for save, submit, copy, review, delete, query/load/retry and input/business validation must use toast. Do not render persistent inline success/error text, colored feedback blocks, banners or Alert/FormItem help containing the operation error. Use Ant Design Vue message for imperative operations or the shared `src/shared/feedback/FeedbackToast.vue` adapter for existing reactive feedback state; do not create another notification implementation or emit both for one action. The adapter uses the installed Ant Design message component and inherits ConfigProvider tokens.

Keep business status and history (e.g. rejected order, failed export record), genuine empty/loading states, field labels and static requirements, and explicit destructive/unsaved-work confirmations. These are content or decision controls, not operation feedback. Preserve all validation and disabled/duplicate-submit protections, drafts and recovery actions. Retry/deposit/verification links remain discoverable after toast dismissal. Preserve `aria-invalid` and associated accessible error descriptions; a visually hidden description may remain, but must not become another visible error panel or a hidden keyboard focus target. Toast must be visible in the current overlay layer, including native dialog top layers, and not steal focus. Coalesce simultaneous field errors, avoid polling/re-render notification spam, clear pending feedback on unmount, and re-notify repeated failed attempts. For new handlers, call toast explicitly per attempt; the reactive adapter requires clearing/changing feedback state for a repeated identical result.

This decision supersedes earlier instructions that require operation-error text to be visibly embedded inside the page/overlay. It does not remove intentional confirmation controls, historical failure details, or validation. Run `npm run check:feedback` for changes touching feedback; test the affected failure/retry flow and report unverified routes/devices.

## Sort weight convention (user decision, 2026-11-27)

## Quant Fund admin (user decision, 2026-11-27)

The new public site's AI Quant → Quant Fund (`/NewQuantCapital/ai-quant/quant-fund`) gets its own admin group 「量化基金」 at `/admin/quant-fund/*` (products, subscription orders, redemption review, payouts, operation logs), separate from the legacy `/admin/ai-quant/*`. Client requirements (confirmed 2026-11-27): subscriptions stay pending review until approval starts interest and the lock period; rejection refunds. Interest accrues inside the order once per full day on principal at a rate inside the product's daily-rate range (equal bounds = fixed rate; different bounds = drawn per day, public shows the average) (no compounding, never credited to the balance) and is paid out with the principal on redemption. Inside the lock period holders may redeem early for a fee of a fixed percentage of principal; after it the order keeps earning until redeemed. Every holder redemption needs admin review and keeps accruing while pending. When funding progress (initial % + active principal) reaches 100%, nobody can subscribe and every order past its lock is auto-redeemed without fee. A per-product restricted UID list shows the product as full to those users. Each product has an identifier coin (`asset`: USDT/USDC/BTC/ETH/SOL) for the public card icon and asset filter only; subscriptions, payouts and redemptions are always USDT. Tag colours follow their type, never per-tag admin configuration: risk badge by level (LOW green, MEDIUM brand accent, HIGH orange, EXTREME red), flexible products automatically get the accent 活期/随时赎回 tags, and free-text strategy tags are always neutral grey. Funding progress and quota use initial funding percentage plus approved principal. The mock runs in-page as static seed data (`src/shared/quantFund/*`), no server or browser persistence; admin and public do not share live state. Follow the strategy admin page conventions. ## C2C admin (user decision, 2026-11-27)

The public C2C (`/NewQuantCapital/c2c/*`) gets its own admin group 「C2C 交易」 at `/admin/c2c/*` (订单审核, 报价管理, 操作日志). Payment accounts are removed: buy and sell orders are submitted for back-office review (`pending_review`); payment and payout happen off-platform. C2C uses real fiat (user decision 2026-11-27): quotes are per coin, fiat and side, price and order amount are in that fiat (USD, EUR, GBP, HKD, SGD, JPY, AUD, CNY), and the user pays / receives it off-platform — an exception to the site-wide USDT denomination; balances stay in coins/USDT. Quotes carry price, single-order limits, validity, note, enabled switch and sort weight (larger first); orders snapshot the quote, new quotes start disabled, saves are versioned and log before/after. The mock is static in-page data (`src/shared/c2c/*`), no persistence; admin and public do not share live state. The 交易协议 page edits one agreement in six language versions (tabs, English first, required and the public fallback; another language is filled completely or left empty); each save creates a new version and logs which languages changed.
